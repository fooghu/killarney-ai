Muckross Abbey

A 15th century Franciscan friary in the heart of the National Park — remarkably intact, with an ancient yew at its centre.

Home›Heritage›Muckross Abbey

Muckross Abbey is one of the best-preserved medieval abbeys in Ireland — and one of the most atmospheric. Founded in 1448 for the Franciscan order, it sits in the woodland of Killarney National Park a short walk from Muckross House. The cloisters, church and tower are remarkably intact for a building of its age, and at the centre of the cloister courtyard grows a yew tree that is said to be as old as the abbey itself.

Muckross Abbey — at a glance

  • Founded 1448 — Franciscan friary
  • Suppressed 1541 — Dissolution of the Monasteries
  • The Yew Tree Ancient — possibly as old as the abbey, 575+ years
  • Location Killarney National Park — 5km from Killarney town
  • Entry Free — open year round

The abbey

The abbey was founded by Donald McCarthy, King of Desmond, for the Franciscan order in 1448. It was suppressed during the Dissolution of the Monasteries under Henry VIII in 1541, but the friars continued to return and use it intermittently for over a century. The building was finally abandoned after Cromwellian forces burned it in 1652. Despite this turbulent history the structure survives remarkably well — the church, tower, cloisters and domestic buildings are all largely intact.

At the centre of the cloister courtyard grows a yew tree that has stood here for over 500 years — longer than most of the ruins around it.

The yew tree

The ancient yew tree growing in the cloister courtyard is the most striking feature of Muckross Abbey. It fills the entire courtyard, its massive trunk and spreading canopy creating a canopy of dark green over the open space. Yew trees are extraordinarily long-lived — this one is believed to be contemporary with the abbey itself, making it over 575 years old. It is one of the most remarkable individual trees in Ireland.

The burial ground

The abbey and its surroundings contain the burial places of many of Kerry's most notable Gaelic families — the MacCarthys, O'Sullivans, O'Donoghues and O'Connells among them. The poet and O'Connell family connection makes it a place of particular significance in Irish cultural history. The graves range from elaborately carved medieval tombstones to simple modern markers.

💡

Local tip: Visit in the evening if you can — the abbey is lit dramatically at dusk and the crowds of the daytime have gone. The atmosphere among the ruins as the light fades is genuinely unforgettable.

Practical information

Getting There

5km from Killarney on the N71 — park at Muckross House and walk 1km through the National Park to the abbey

Entry

Free — open year round, no booking required

Best Time

Evening visits are magical — the abbey is quieter and the light on the stone is exceptional at dusk

Nearby

Muckross House and Gardens (1km), Torc Waterfall (2km), Reenadinna Yew Wood (2km)
