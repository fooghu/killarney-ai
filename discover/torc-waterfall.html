Torc Waterfall

Fifteen minutes through ancient oak woodland to one of Ireland's most beautiful waterfalls.

Home › Discover › Torc Waterfall

Torc Waterfall is one of those rare places that delivers exactly what it promises — and then some. A short, well-maintained path from the car park leads through ancient oak woodland, the sound of the river building as you go, until the waterfall suddenly comes into full view. At 20 metres high it's not the tallest in Ireland, but the setting — dense woodland, the mossy gorge, the Owengarriff River tumbling down from the Torc Mountain plateau above — makes it one of the most atmospheric.

Torc Waterfall — at a glance

  • Height 20 metres
  • Walk in 10–15 minutes from car park
  • Difficulty Easy — well-surfaced path, suitable for all
  • River Owengarriff River, flowing from Torc Mountain
  • Location Muckross Road, 8km from Killarney town
  • Entry Free — open year round

The walk

The path from the car park to the waterfall is well-surfaced and takes around 10 to 15 minutes at a comfortable pace. It runs alongside the Owengarriff River through a gorge of ancient oak woodland — the trees here are part of Killarney's native forest, one of the last surviving examples of pre-settlement Irish woodland. In wet weather the whole gorge glistens with moss and the river runs fast and loud.

At the waterfall itself, stone steps lead up to a viewing platform at the base of the cascade. The waterfall drops in two main stages — the upper fall through a narrow rock channel, the lower fall spreading wide before meeting the river pool below. After heavy rain it's genuinely powerful.

💡

Local tip: Most visitors see only the lower waterfall and turn back. If you continue up the stone steps to the right of the falls you reach a second viewpoint looking back over Muckross Lake and the mountains — one of the best views in the National Park and largely missed by the crowds below.

Extending the walk

Torc Waterfall works well as a standalone 30-minute visit, but it also connects to longer routes through the National Park. The steps above the waterfall continue up the southern slope of Torc Mountain, eventually reaching the Old Kenmare Road — a historic trackway that crosses the mountains between Killarney and Kenmare with outstanding views over the Upper Lake.

🥾

Waterfall Only

⏱ 30 min 📏 1.5km return 🟢 Easy

Car park to waterfall and back. Well-surfaced path, suitable for all ages and abilities including buggies as far as the viewing area.

🏔️

Waterfall + Upper Viewpoint

⏱ 1 hr 📏 3km return 🟡 Moderate

Continue up the stone steps above the falls to reach the panoramic viewpoint over Muckross Lake. Steeper but very rewarding — most visitors miss this entirely.

🗺️

Old Kenmare Road Loop

⏱ 3–4 hr 📏 12km 🟠 Challenging

A full day loop via the Old Kenmare Road across the mountains — outstanding views, genuine wilderness, and historically significant as a pre-famine route.

🚴

By Bike from Killarney

⏱ 45 min each way 📏 8km from town 🟢 Easy

Cycle from Killarney town through the National Park to the Torc car park. The park roads are largely car-free and the cycling is excellent.

Most visitors see the waterfall and turn back. The steps above it lead to one of the best views in the entire National Park — and almost nobody goes up.

Best time to visit

Torc Waterfall is at its most dramatic after heavy rainfall, when the Owengarriff River is running full and the falls are loud and powerful. In dry summer weather the flow reduces noticeably. The woodland is beautiful year round — vivid green in spring and summer, golden in autumn, and dramatically atmospheric on a winter morning with mist in the gorge.

Summer mornings before 10am are the quietest time to visit. By midday in July and August the car park fills and the path to the waterfall gets busy. Arriving early or going in the evening avoids most of the crowds.

Practical information

Getting There

8km from Killarney town on the N71 Muckross Road. Car park at the trailhead. Cycleable from town through the park

Parking

Free car park at the trailhead. Can fill quickly in summer — arrive early or cycle from town

Entry

Free — open year round, no booking required

Facilities

No café or toilets at the waterfall — nearest facilities at Muckross House, 2km away

Accessibility

The main path to the lower viewpoint is surfaced and accessible. The steps above the falls are steep and uneven

What to Wear

The path can be wet and slippery after rain — good footwear recommended. A waterproof layer is always wise in Kerry
